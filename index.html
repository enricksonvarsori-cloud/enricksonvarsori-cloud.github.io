Research in Practice

How research moves beyond the page

My work often sits between research, technology, institutions and the people expected to use, shape or respond to scientific knowledge.

Engaging people in research

Research becomes stronger when those affected by it help shape it.

I design and contribute to stakeholder engagement, co-creation and participatory processes that connect research teams with communities, practitioners and institutional actors.

Evaluating technology in context

Usability matters most when technologies meet real users and real situations.

My work combines human–computer interaction, usability and user experience evaluation with applied settings, including digital platforms, educational games and participatory tools.

Translating science into action

Research has greater value when findings can travel beyond academic outputs.

I work on science communication, exploitation, training and uptake strategies that help scientific results reach decision-makers, professionals and wider publics.

Connecting disciplines and institutions

Complex problems require research that moves across boundaries.

My work spans communication, sustainability, HCI, education and emerging technologies within international and interdisciplinary research environments.

About

A short academic biography

Enrickson Varsori is a researcher in Communication Sciences whose work explores how people interact with technologies, institutions and contemporary social and environmental challenges. His research spans digital media, hyperconnectivity and temporal experience, human–computer interaction, sustainability, science communication and emerging technologies, combining quantitative, qualitative and participatory approaches.

He currently works at the University of Coimbra within the Faculty of Sciences and Technology and is an Integrated Researcher at the Communication and Society Research Centre (CECS), University of Minho. His trajectory has been developed across European and international research projects and academic networks, including work in Portugal, Brazil and broader international collaborations. Across these contexts, his research is driven by a common question: how can communication and technology be better understood, evaluated and mobilised in ways that are meaningful to people and society?
