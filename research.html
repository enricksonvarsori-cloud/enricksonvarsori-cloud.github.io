Research

Questions at the intersection of communication, technology and society.

My research examines how people experience technological change, how digital systems shape everyday life, and how research can be evaluated, communicated and translated into meaningful practice.

Research landscape

Four interconnected areas.

01

Digital life & temporalities

How does continuous digital connection reshape attention, time and everyday social experience?

My work examines hyperconnectivity, subjective online time, digital practices and the ways young people experience increasingly continuous technological environments.

Hyperconnectivity Subjective time Digital media Youth
02

HCI & usability

How should digital systems be evaluated when use is situated, social and context-dependent?

I study usability, user experience and interaction with digital platforms, interfaces and technology-mediated systems, with an emphasis on evaluation in real-world contexts.

Usability User experience Interfaces Evaluation
03

Sustainability & participation

How can scientific knowledge become more usable, participatory and meaningful for the people expected to act on it?

This line of work brings together environmental communication, ocean literacy, stakeholder engagement, public participation and the societal uptake of research.

Environmental communication Stakeholders Participation Ocean literacy
04

AI & emerging technologies

What happens when new technological systems begin to reorganise communication, judgement and everyday practices?

I am interested in the human, ethical and communicative dimensions of artificial intelligence and generative technologies, particularly where they intersect with education, time and social experience.

Generative AI Ethics Communication Emerging technology

Across these areas

The recurring questions are often the same.

Experience

How do people live with technology?

Looking beyond access or adoption to attention, perception, routines, emotions and social meaning.

Evaluation

How do we know whether a technology works for its users?

Combining usability, user experience and contextual evaluation rather than relying on isolated metrics.

Translation

How does research move into practice?

Examining communication, stakeholder engagement and the conditions that shape meaningful uptake.

Research approach

Methods follow the question.

I work across quantitative, qualitative, mixed-method and participatory designs. Depending on the research problem, this can include surveys, psychometric measures, interviews, content and policy analysis, systematic review, usability and user-experience evaluation, workshops and stakeholder engagement.

The common principle is contextual fit: choosing methods that make sense for the people, technologies and institutional environments being studied.
